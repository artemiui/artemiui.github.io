## Abstract

Static molecular docking is cheap. Molecular dynamics is not. The gap between them is where a pre-screen earns its keep: if you can narrow a library of candidate compounds down to a handful before committing anything to an expensive simulation — or a bench — you have saved the part of the pipeline that actually costs money.

This project builds that pre-screen for **phytochemicals**, plant-derived compounds of the sort found in fruit, tea, and medicinal plants. 33 ligands drawn from Fatma, Jameel & Siddique (2023) were docked against 90 protein targets spanning 35 biological hallmarks, producing 7,619 scored ligand–pocket pairs. Each compound carries both a binding affinity and a composite toxicity score, so candidates can be ranked on the two objectives jointly rather than one at a time.

Four compounds clear both medians with margin: **rutin**, **(−)-epigallocatechin**, **α-amyrin**, and **paeoniflorin**.

## Method

The pipeline runs in five stages, all reproducible from public tools:

1. **Binding site detection.** [P2Rank](https://github.com/rdk/p2rank) ranks candidate pockets across each protein structure, which avoids having to hand-specify a docking box per target.
2. **Docking.** Each ligand is docked into the top three pockets of every protein with [AutoDock Vina](https://vina.scripps.edu/) 1.2.7, ten poses per pocket, with only the best pose carried forward.
3. **Toxicity and pharmacokinetics.** [ADMET-AI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae416) predicts roughly 40 endpoints per compound, rolled into a weighted `Composite_Tox_Score`.
4. **Hallmark annotation.** Each protein is assigned the biological process it stands in for, so results can be read at the level of biology rather than individual PDB entries.
5. **Analysis.** Exploratory statistics, frontier search, and supervised models in scikit-learn and XGBoost.

The dataset was self-curated over roughly two days and is not drawn from any existing public benchmark.

### The scoring function

Vina's affinity score is an empirically weighted sum of five interaction terms with a torsional penalty:

$$
E_{\text{vina}} = w_1\,\text{gauss}_1 + w_2\,\text{gauss}_2 + w_3\,\text{repulsion} + w_4\,\text{hydrophobic} + w_5\,\text{Hbond} + w_6 \cdot N_{\text{rot}}
$$

where $N_{\text{rot}}$ is the number of rotatable bonds and the $w_i$ are fitted weights. The result approximates binding free energy in kcal/mol, with more negative meaning tighter binding.

## Results

**The frontier holds.** Four compounds sit below both the median affinity (−6.00 kcal/mol) and the median composite toxicity (0.34). Rutin has the strongest mean affinity in the set at −6.58 kcal/mol alongside the second-lowest toxicity score — though at 610 Da with a single Lipinski pass, its oral bioavailability is a genuine problem that the docking score alone will not tell you about.

**No single descriptor predicts affinity.** Across eleven physicochemical and ADMET descriptors, nothing clears $r = 0.4$ against binding affinity. `logP` — the descriptor most often assumed to track binding — sits at $r = 0.02$. The one strong relationship in the panel, CYP3A4 inhibition against toxicity at $r = 0.77$, is close to circular: CYP inhibition is itself one of the endpoints rolled into the composite score.

**The models split.** Tuned XGBoost and SVR reach $R^2 \approx 0.999$ on binding affinity with $\text{RMSE} < 0.04$. That is not a result to celebrate — it is leakage. The feature matrix still contained `mean_affinity`, which correlates with the target at $r = -0.97$, so the models are largely reading the answer off an adjacent column. A clean-featured gradient-boosting run on physicochemical and ADMET descriptors alone reaches $R^2 = 0.188$, and a refined random forest the same. **That** number is what 2D descriptors are worth for binding affinity.

Classifying the biological hallmark from molecular properties reaches 0.21 accuracy across 35 classes. Low in absolute terms, but roughly seven times a uniform-random baseline of 0.029 — weak signal rather than no signal. Molecular properties dictate binding thermodynamics; they are only a fraction of what maps a molecule to a broad biological process.

## Limitations

- **Static docking only.** No dynamics, so no entropy, no solvent, no induced fit. Vina scores are a pre-screen, not binding free energies.
- **Toxicity is predicted, not measured.** The composite score is a roll-up of model outputs, and its discrete banding is visible as horizontal striping in the frontier plot.
- **Hallmark labels are editorial.** Each protein stands in for a process by assignment, so hallmark-level medians inherit that choice as much as they reflect chemistry.
- **One pose per pocket.** Only the best of ten poses is carried forward, discarding the ensemble a dynamics run would use.

None of this sinks the pipeline — it sets what the pipeline is for.

## Future work
