## Divergence Test

As preliminary, every series must undergo preliminary screening via the $n$th-Term Divergence Test:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n$$

- If $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges, then necessarily $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$.
- If $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \ne 0$ or the limit does not exist, the series **diverges immediately**.
- ($(p \rightarrow q) \neq (q \rightarrow p)$ The converse is *strictly false*. The condition $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$ is merely necessary, not sufficient (e.g., the harmonic series $\lim \frac{1}{n} = 0$, yet $\sum \frac{1}{n} = \infty$). When the limit vanishes, the test is inconclusive and the series must pass through the structural dispatch gate.

## Special Series Forms

Certain canonical series possess closed-form algebraic expressions whose convergence behavior is completely characterized by special cases. Such are the following:

### Geometric Series
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty a r^{n-1} = a + ar + ar^2 + \dots$$
- **Convergence Criterion**: $|r| < 1$, converging analytically to the sum:
  $$S = \frac{a}{1 - r}$$
- **Divergence Criterion**: $|r| \ge 1$.

### p-Series
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^p} = \frac{1}{1^p} + \frac{1}{2^p} + \frac{1}{3^p} + \dots$$
- **Convergence**: $p > 1$.
- **Divergence**: $0 < p \le 1$.
- **Harmonic Series ($p = 1$)**: $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n} = \infty$.

### Telescoping Sums
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty (b_n - b_{n+k})$$
- Evaluated by partial fraction decomposition and intermediate cancellation of terms in the sequence of partial sums $S_m = \sum_{j=1}^k b_j - \sum_{j=1}^k b_{m+j}$, converging if and only if $\lim_{m \to \infty} b_m$ exists as a finite real limit.

## Nonnegative Terms ($a_n \ge 0$)

When terms are strictly nonnegative, the sequence of partial sums $\{S_k\}$ is monotonically non-decreasing ($S_{k+1} \ge S_k$). By the Monotone Convergence Theorem, convergence is equivalent to boundedness above.

### Cauchy Integral Test
Let $a_n = f(n)$, where $f(x)$ is continuous, positive, and decreasing on $[k, \infty)$:
$$\int_k^\infty f(x) \, dx < \infty \iff \sum_{n=k}^\infty a_n < \infty$$

### Direct Comparison Test (DCT)
Let $0 \le a_n \le b_n$ for all $n \ge k$:
- If $\sum b_n$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges.
- If $\sum a_n$ diverges, then $\sum b_n$ diverges.

### Limit Comparison Test (LCT)
For $a_n > 0$ and $b_n > 0$, evaluate the asymptotic ratio:
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n}$$
- If $0 < L < \infty$, then both series $\sum a_n$ and $\sum b_n$ share identical convergence status.
- If $L = 0$ and $\sum b_n$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges.
- If $L = \infty$ and $\sum b_n$ diverges, then $\sum a_n$ diverges.

## Alternating Series

### Leibniz Alternating Series Test (AST)
For series of the form $\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^{n-1} b_n$ with $b_n > 0$:
1. $\{b_n\}$ is ultimately monotonically decreasing: $b_{n+1} \le b_n$ for all $n \ge k$.
2. $\lim_{n \to \infty} b_n = 0$.

If both hypotheses are satisfied, the series **converges**, with truncation error bounded by the magnitude of the first neglected term:
$$|R_n| = |S - S_n| \le b_{n+1}$$

## Absolute Convergence Ratio & Root Tests

### Conditional vs. Absolute Convergence
- **Absolute Convergence**: $\sum |a_n| < \infty \implies \sum a_n < \infty$. Absolute convergence permits arbitrary rearrangement of terms without altering the sum (Dirichlet's Theorem).
- **Conditional Convergence**: $\sum a_n$ converges via sign cancellation, but $\sum |a_n| = \infty$. By the Riemann Series Theorem, conditional series can be rearranged to converge to any arbitrary real number or diverge.


### d'Alembert Ratio Test
Ideal for terms containing factorials ($n!$) and exponential bases ($c^n$):
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left| \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} \right|$$
- $L < 1 \implies$ **Absolutely Convergent**.
- $L > 1$ or $L = \infty \implies$ **Divergent**.
- $L = 1 \implies$ **Inconclusive** (fails for all rational expressions and $p$-series).

### Cauchy Root Test
Ideal for terms containing nested $n$th powers $(g(n))^n$:
$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sqrt[n]{|a_n|} = \lim_{n \to \infty} |a_n|^{1/n}$$
- $L < 1 \implies$ **Absolutely Convergent**.
- $L > 1$ or $L = \infty \implies$ **Divergent**.
- $L = 1 \implies$ **Inconclusive**.

## Power Series & Analytic Expansions

### Radius ($R$) and Interval of Convergence (IoC)
For a power series centered at $x = a$:
$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n (x - a)^n$$

The radius of convergence $R$ is extracted via the Ratio or Cauchy-Hadamard formula:
$$R = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left| \frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}} \right| \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{1}{R} = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \sqrt[n]{|c_n|}$$

- The series converges absolutely on $(a - R, a + R)$ and diverges on $|x - a| > R$.
- The endpoints $x = a - R$ and $x = a + R$ must be tested individually using non-power tests to determine inclusive brackets (`[` or `]`).

### Taylor & Maclaurin Series
$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!} (x - a)^n$$
A function $f(x)$ is analytic on $(a-R, a+R)$ if its Taylor remainder vanishes asymptotically:
$$\lim_{k \to \infty} R_k(x) = 0$$

## Decision Matrix Reference

We summarize the ideas in this article and diagram through the following decision matrix.

| Test Name | Typical Form / Target | Convergence Condition | Divergence Condition | Inconclusive Boundary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Divergence Test** | Any general series $\sum a_n$ | — (Never proves convergence) | $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \ne 0$ or DNE | $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$ |
| **Geometric Series** | $\sum a r^{n-1}$ | $\|r\| < 1 \implies S = \frac{a}{1-r}$ | $\|r\| \ge 1$ | None |
| **$p$-Series** | $\sum \frac{1}{n^p}$ | $p > 1$ | $0 < p \le 1$ | None |
| **Integral Test** | Continuous, positive, decreasing $f(x)$ | $\int_k^\infty f(x)\,dx < \infty$ | $\int_k^\infty f(x)\,dx = \infty$ | Non-monotonic or oscillatory $f(x)$ |
| **Direct Comparison** | Nonnegative $a_n, b_n \ge 0$ | $a_n \le b_n$ and $\sum b_n < \infty$ | $a_n \ge b_n$ and $\sum b_n = \infty$ | Wrong inequality orientation |
| **Limit Comparison** | Positive rational / algebraic terms | $0 < \lim \frac{a_n}{b_n} < \infty$ and $\sum b_n < \infty$ | $0 < \lim \frac{a_n}{b_n} < \infty$ and $\sum b_n = \infty$ | $L = 0$ (with div $b_n$) or $L = \infty$ (with conv $b_n$) |
| **Alternating (AST)** | $\sum (-1)^{n-1} b_n$ ($b_n > 0$) | $b_{n+1} \le b_n$ and $\lim b_n = 0$ | $\lim b_n \ne 0$ | Non-monotonic vanishing $b_n$ |
| **Ratio Test** | Factorials ($n!$) & exponentials ($c^n$) | $L = \lim \left\|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right\| < 1$ | $L > 1$ or $L = \infty$ | $L = 1$ |
| **Root Test** | Nested $n$th powers $(g(n))^n$ | $L = \lim \sqrt[n]{\|a_n\|} < 1$ | $L > 1$ or $L = \infty$ | $L = 1$ |
| **Power Series** | $\sum c_n (x-a)^n$ | $\|x - a\| < R$ | $\|x - a\| > R$ | Endpoints $x = a \pm R$ require testing |
