Today is World Philosophy Day.

It is not a seldom occurrence that I am questioned about my interest in academic philosophy despite studying statistics (as a fun fact, it is included in my letter to the Dean that I likened myself to Bayes in that regard. It's an ambitious comparison, I know). 

My position is that all students in a the natural sciences, the social sciences, or in the arts should be knowledgeable about the fundamental bases of their respective fields. 

We create drastic assumptions about how everything works before we consider it knowledge.  You can trust in how a certain system functions, build great things from it as an engineer, but you can never truly claim that it is absolute truth when the system becomes stochastic a few zooms later. It's why mathematicians hate the, as mentioned, assumption to "let $\pi = 3$" so much. You're leaving too much information out. 

"But it works, so why bother?"

Well, for one, we are curious creatures. You may argue the case that we've stopped being curious as a general consensus, but nonetheless, outliers still push through the limits of the domain of knowledge just for the sake of it. The domain itself is the pleasure even if it is through an incorrect theory or interpretation. Philosophy is a fun 'game' in-itself!

"AI is going to take all our jobs, and all philosophy is, is word porn dressed as intellectualism."

When Language Models provide a conjecture in a googol lines of Lean, it is no better than our preconceived understanding of the counterclaim conjecture without AI. That is, of course, not knowing it at all. 

'Extending' the domain of a field is greater than just tweaking a variable. Philosophy is ideation, argumentation, and conceptualizing; at least depending on which side of the archipelago you're on. To break the fundamental assumptions and create drastic theories for explaining how the world works. May that be in your scope of experience, or deductively through evidence. 

The choice is yours, because in its entirety it's not really word-porn-dressed-as-intellectualism, moreso your philosophy narrowed down to the scope that beloved university-degree-field wants from you.

So even if you consider it a useless degree, I thank you for reading this short excerpt, fellow philosopher.
