It is reasonable to be accepting of industrialization—most especially in a country that is in dire need of it, as is advocated by the esteemed Mr. Stephen CuUnjieng. It is unreasonable, however, to have figures be like Atty. Wilfredo Garrido who pushes narratives of overwhelming positivity to the non-existing transparency of Pax Silica.

Industrialization is no boogey-man, but it comes, of course, with its own mishaps. Mr. CuUnjieng showcases a healthy skepticism of the case it be if the ~1GW—he denotes 3000MW—data center be constructed in the Philippines. It is impossible to sustain livelihood for the regular Filipino if connected to the current grid. Developments by the likes of MTerra would not be sufficient given the limited capacity of solar. 

Well then, what of nuclear energy?

It seems that the LGUs of Bataan are openly against the revitalization of the BNP, so what options do we have?

The people of the Philippines do not want Pax Silica for the following reasons. This I try to list down to my capacity, but given the complexity, there are, by all certainty, more reasons this policy gets a rejection from the common Filipino.

1. The energy of the country (~14GW) simply cannot sustain it.
2. The AI data center cannot be built on the Philippines.
3. If no sufficient compensation or support are given to farmers, or worse, IPs are forcibly displaced, then it treats people as means to an end of capital.
4. Its timing clearly is a means of extracting relevant minerals for empowering the already tensive bubble of the LLM market in the United States.
5. The country is, once again, being used as a means to an end as a mere vantage point over the neighboring superpower China.

The lack of visible transparency showcases ulterior motive—obviously as a means to show its enemy that it has the high ground now.

Why Atty. Garrido seems to push this narrative is not entirely out of place or unreasonable. In Constantino's work "The Miseducation of the Filipino", it is clear that we have been delimited to the perspective that we are unable to create manufacturing within our own soil. 

In other words, we were subdued by the Americans to the idea that we were merely farmers incapable of technological prowess. This is, by no means, an attack on agriculture, of course. But we can be more than it. We were simply tamed through the beautiful romanticization of the agricultural life that we have grown accustomed to it and are groomed to believe that this is all we are.

Why Mr. CuUnjieng seems to accept it as it is now, is because it seems like the awaited golden goose, even if he still remains with his reservations, rightfully so.

The opportunity has come—Pax Silica! However, it has come from the same people who groomed us into believing that we could have never become to industrialize. That is the irony.

We will only welcome industrialization when the conditions are clear and the people of the Philippines are not taken advantage of once again. No to Pax Silica.
