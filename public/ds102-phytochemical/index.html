ML-based phytochemical analysis for multi-objective optimization with efficacy and toxicity metrics

Artemio S. Arcega III · UP Data Science Society Bootcamp · April 2026
molecular docking ADMET multi-objective optimization AutoDock Vina P2Rank

A self-curated screen of 33 plant-derived compounds against 90 protein targets, scored for binding strength and predicted toxicity at once. This page is the interactive rendition of the notebook: every figure below is drawn from the same 7,619-row dataset the paper reports on, and every mark is yours to interrogate.

00

What was measured

Binding sites were located with P2Rank, each ligand docked into the top three pockets of every protein with AutoDock Vina, and toxicity plus pharmacokinetics predicted with ADMET-AI. Two numbers drive everything that follows:

  • best_affinity — the strongest Vina pose for a ligand–pocket pair, in kcal/mol. More negative means tighter binding.
  • Composite_Tox_Score — a weighted roll-up of 20-odd ADMET toxicity endpoints. Lower means safer.

The objective is a frontier: compounds that bind hard and score clean. Neither metric alone is a drug candidate, which is exactly why the two have to be read on one plane.

The efficacy–toxicity frontier

Each dot is one of the 33 phytochemicals, placed by its average binding affinity across all 90 targets and its composite toxicity score. The dashed lines are the medians of each axis; the shaded quadrant is the one worth caring about — stronger than median binding, safer than median toxicity. Hover any dot for its full profile; click to pin it.

What the frontier finds

Rutin (CID 5280805), (−)-epigallocatechin (CID 72277), α-amyrin (CID 73170) and paeoniflorin (CID 442534) clear both medians. Rutin is the standout: the strongest mean affinity in the set at −6.58 kcal/mol with the second-lowest toxicity score — though its molecular weight of 610 Da and single Lipinski pass are a real liability for oral bioavailability.

Which biology binds hardest

Every protein in the set carries a hallmark label — the biological process it stands in for. Grouping all 7,619 docking scores by hallmark shows where this chemical library has leverage and where it does not. Boxes are the interquartile range, the line is the median, whiskers run to 1.5× IQR.

04

What actually predicts affinity

If a cheap molecular descriptor predicted binding affinity, screening would be free. This is the correlation of eleven physicochemical and ADMET descriptors against the two scaled objectives. Bars run right for positive correlation, left for negative.

This is the paper's quiet finding. logP, the descriptor most often assumed to track binding, sits at r = 0.02. Multi-parameter modelling is not a stylistic preference here; no single descriptor carries the signal.

05

Where the models succeed, and where they do not

Two learning tasks were run on the same feature matrix: regress the binding affinity, and classify the biological hallmark. The results split so sharply that the gap is the result.

The honest version

A clean-featured gradient-boosting run on physicochemical and ADMET descriptors alone reaches R² = 0.188 — and a refined random forest, 0.188 as well. That number, not the 0.999, is what 2D descriptors are worth for binding affinity. Resolving the rest needs 3D structural or graph representations.

07

Limits worth stating

  • Static docking only. No molecular dynamics, so no entropy, no solvent, no induced fit. Vina scores are a pre-screen, not binding free energies.
  • Toxicity is predicted, not measured. Composite_Tox_Score is a roll-up of model outputs; its discrete banding is visible in the frontier plot as horizontal stripes.
  • Hallmark labels are assignments. Each protein stands in for a process by editorial choice, so hallmark-level medians inherit that choice.
  • One pose per pocket. Only the best of ten poses per pocket is carried forward, which discards the ensemble a dynamics run would use.

None of this sinks the pipeline. It sets what the pipeline is for: narrowing 33 × 90 pairs to a handful worth the expensive simulation.
